Jump to a screen

    Everything inside the phone works: swipe from the left edge to go back, drag panels down, turn the ring on Categories.

    Twelve screens,
    two themes

    From the first launch to the settings page, every screen a real budgeting app needs. Tap one to open it in the live prototype.

    Motion that explains

    Every animation shows where a number came from or where you just went. Nothing moves just to fill the screen.

    A ring you can turn

    Drag the ring and it settles on the nearest slice with a spring. The centre swaps to that category, and its row lights up below.

    Charts that draw in order

    October goes first as a faint line, then November traces over it and a dotted forecast runs to the end of the month. Tap a bar for that day's total.

    Reading a card

    Pick a bank and the card lands, a light sweeps across it, and Pulse counts the spends it found before filing them into categories.

    Screens slide, the old one steps back

    A new screen comes in from the right in 460 ms. The one behind shrinks and dims, so you always know where back goes.

    Springs for things you touch

    Buttons, switches, the tab bar and the ring all overshoot slightly and settle, like real objects.

    Numbers count up

    Totals roll from zero when a screen opens and from the old value when you change something.

    The light moves with you

    The aurora behind the app drifts slowly and shifts to a new place on every screen. Switching themes spreads out from your finger.

    Four directions, one winner

    Before building twelve screens I drew the same two screens in four styles, with the same data. Open any of them to see it move.

    Aurora glassChosen

    Frosted panels floating over a slow violet and coral aurora.

    Colour blocks

    Solid, saturated tiles packed tight, with no glass or shadows.

    Soft depth

    Pulse line

    It carries the ad

    The ad glows. Aurora glass keeps that light behind every screen, so the app feels like the product the ad promised.

    It works in light mode

    The same panels turn milky white over a pastel aurora. Some of the other directions lost their character without the dark.

    It lets colour mean something

    Neutral glass leaves the four category colours as the loudest thing on screen, which is exactly where your eye should go.
